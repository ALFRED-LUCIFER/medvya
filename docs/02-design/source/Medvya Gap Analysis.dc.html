DMedvya · GAP ANALYSIS · UAE + INDIA

What a clinic would still miss, and how to close it

Reviewed from three seats: the doctor in the room, the receptionist at the front desk, and the owner deciding whether to buy. The biggest gaps are outside patient history and moving old data out of legacy systems such as Oracle. Each item is marked Must (blocks a sale), Should (wins the sale) or Later.

MUST SHOULD LATER

1 · Patient history from outside the clinic

Today the doctor sees only what was recorded in this clinic. Patients arrive with WhatsApp PDFs, hospital discharge papers and records held by the emirate’s health exchange. Medvya should pull these in automatically and label every item with its source.

UAE health exchange pull and pushMUST

Search by Emirates ID at check-in and pull allergies, diagnoses, medicines, labs and discharge summaries from Malaffi (Abu Dhabi), NABIDH (Dubai) or Riayati (northern emirates). Push every signed visit back. Licensed facilities are expected to connect, so a clinic cannot switch without it.

Connects via: HL7 v2 and FHIR interfaces after each regulator’s onboarding and conformance testing. One integration module, three configurations.
Old reports uploaded by the patientMUST

Patient sends PDFs or photos through the portal or a WhatsApp link before the visit. OCR and AI extract lab values, dates and diagnoses into the trend charts, tagged “Unverified · patient upload” until the doctor accepts them in one click.

Connects via: existing AI worker queue + Azure Document Intelligence. Original file always kept next to extracted values.
India · ABHA consent-based recordsSHOULD

Patient scans the clinic’s ABHA QR at reception, approves a consent request on their phone, and linked records from other ABDM facilities appear in the chart for the consented period.

Connects via: ABDM Health Information Provider and User APIs (FHIR bundles), certified through the ABDM sandbox.
External lab and imaging feedsSHOULD

Results from reference labs land in the results inbox automatically. Radiology reports arrive as text; images open in the provider’s viewer through a link instead of being copied.

Connects via: HL7 ORU for labs, DICOMweb viewer links for imaging.
Clinic inbox for lettersLATER

A dedicated email address per clinic. Referral letters and discharge summaries sent there are matched to the patient and filed, with a “needs filing” queue for anything unmatched.

Connects via: inbound mail parser + same patient-matching service used for migration.

2 · Moving old data from Oracle and other systems

Most clinics will not switch if they lose their history or have to stop work for a weekend. The fastest safe approach is to move what is needed on day one, keep both systems in sync while staff get used to Medvya, then load the rest in the background.

OPTION A · FULL MIGRATION FIRST Slowest, highest risk

Every record is cleaned and moved before go-live. Weeks of data fixing up front, then a hard cutover. Problems found after cutover are hard to undo.

OPTION B · RECENT FIRST + LIVE SYNC · RECOMMENDED Go live in 1–2 weeks

Load active patients and open items first. Change-data-capture keeps Oracle and Medvya in step during a parallel run. Older history and documents follow in the background.

OPTION C · LINK ONLY Fastest, but split history

Nothing is moved; a “View in old system” link opens the legacy record. Useful as a fallback for very old data, not as the main plan.

Option B step by step

1Connect read-onlyRead-only database user on the clinic’s Oracle server. Azure Data Factory with a self-hosted integration runtime on a clinic PC pulls tables over an encrypted tunnel, so no ports open to the internet. Data lands in a raw staging schema in Azure UAE North (or India Central).
2Map with AI, approve by handAI proposes column mappings and code mappings (local test names to LOINC, diagnoses to ICD-10, drugs to the country formulary). A person approves each mapping once. Approved mappings are saved as a template for that legacy vendor, so the next clinic on the same system takes days instead of weeks.
3Dry run and reportFull trial load into a test tenant. The owner gets a report before signing off: patient counts, duplicates found, records with missing Emirates ID or ABHA, values that failed checks. The same load can be re-run any number of times.
4Load what day one needsPatients seen in the last 24 months, future appointments, outstanding balances, active medicines, allergies, problem list and last results. This is usually a small share of the database and loads in hours.
5Parallel run with live syncChange-data-capture (Oracle LogMiner via Debezium, or GoldenGate if the clinic already licenses it) streams new rows from Oracle into Medvya for 1–2 weeks. Staff can fall back to the old system at any time. Cut over when the front desk is comfortable.
6Backfill the restOlder visits load overnight in batches. Scanned paper and old PDFs are indexed first (patient, date, type) and OCR runs only when a doctor opens them. The legacy database stays read-only for an agreed period.

Making corrections fast

ruleFix by rule, not by rowA data-quality queue groups problems by type. “Glucose in mg/dL stored as mmol/L” is fixed once and applied to all matching rows, then re-run.
groupDuplicate matchingScores likely duplicates on Emirates ID, phone, date of birth and name, including Arabic–English spellings (Mohammed / Muhammad). High-confidence pairs merge in bulk; borderline ones are confirmed by the receptionist when the patient is at the desk.
historyReversible and traceableEvery imported value keeps its source system, original ID and import date, shown as an “Imported” badge. Merges and bulk fixes can be undone and are in the audit log.
table_viewNon-Oracle sources tooSame pipeline for SQL Server, MySQL, Excel/CSV exports and HL7 or FHIR exports from other EMRs. Only the first step changes.

6 · Voice appointment assistant

MUST · NEW

Already in Medvya: ambient scribe and the doctor’s private voice assistant (Clinical Screens A-07). Missing: an assistant that answers the clinic phone and WhatsApp voice notes, books, moves and cancels appointments, and hands over to reception when needed. Missed calls are lost bookings, and the front desk is busiest exactly when patients call.

What it does
callAnswers 24/7 on the clinic number, after hours and when all lines are busy. Arabic, English, Hindi and Malayalam; switches language mid-call.
event_availableBooks from the live calendar. Offers real free slots by doctor, specialty, branch and language. Holds the slot while the caller decides.
badgeIdentifies the caller by phone number, then confirms date of birth or last 4 digits of Emirates ID / ABHA before sharing any appointment detail.
health_and_safetyChecks insurance on the call and tells the caller whether the visit is covered and the expected co-pay.
edit_calendarReschedule, cancel, waitlist. Fills cancelled slots by calling or messaging the waitlist.
campaignOutbound calls. Reminder and confirmation calls for patients who don’t use WhatsApp, recall calls for follow-ups due.
infoRoutine questions: timings, location, parking, fasting before tests, accepted insurers.

Safety limitsNo medical advice, triage or results over the phone. Emergency words trigger the emergency number script and an immediate transfer. Always says it is an automated assistant.
Handover rulesTransfers when the caller asks for a person, after two failed attempts, for complaints, billing disputes or VIP patients. Staff see the transcript and summary before picking up.
Owner controlsOn/off per branch and hour, which doctors it can book, greeting and voice, call recording consent wording, and a weekly report of bookings and missed revenue recovered.
How it connectsAzure Communication Services for the phone number and SIP trunk to the clinic’s existing line; speech and language models in-region; books through the same PMS appointment API as reception, so rules and audit are identical.
